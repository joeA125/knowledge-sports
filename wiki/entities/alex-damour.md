# Alex D'Amour

PhD candidate in the Statistics Department at Harvard University at the time of publication. Co-author of [[multiresolution-stochastic-process-nba-possessions|A Multiresolution Stochastic Process Model for Predicting Basketball Possession Outcomes]] (2016), which introduced [[martingale-epv]], and — the same year — of [[meta-analytics-sports-metrics|Meta-Analytics]], with Franks, Cervone and Bornn.

He is a **Neyman Visiting Assistant Professor** in the Department of Statistics at UC Berkeley at the time of the second paper.

**Two held sources.**

## The Only Vault Author Whose Stated Interests Match the Vault's Biggest Gap

> **Added 2026-08-29** on ingest of [[meta-analytics-sports-metrics|Franks et al. (2016)]].

This page already recorded his interests as spanning causal inference, [[bayesian-inference|Bayesian]] statistics, **and the reliability of machine learning systems** — a note written before the vault held anything of his on reliability.

It now does. [[meta-analytics-sports-metrics|Meta-Analytics]] defines [[metric-discrimination|discrimination]], [[metric-stability|stability]] and [[metric-independence|independence]], and supplies the [[metric-variance-components|variance decomposition]] underneath all three.

⚠️ **Unlike [[luke-bornn|Bornn]] and [[daniel-cervone|Cervone]], D'Amour has no football paper in this vault**, so the `the-framework-and-the-metrics-share-authors-and-never-meet` observation does not attach to him. He is the one member of that group who wrote the framework and did not go on to build a football metric that ignores it.

That makes him the cleanest **lead into the reliability-of-ML-systems literature**, which [[reliability-layers]] argues is where a closing source for the vault's top priority is most likely to come from — with the caveat that the "outside the field" reading was itself overturned by this ingest.
