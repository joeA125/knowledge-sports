# Alexander Franks

First author of [[meta-analytics-sports-metrics|Meta-Analytics (2016)]], which defines **discrimination, stability and independence** as measurable properties of a sports metric — the framework the vault's top acquisition priority requires.

At the time of writing: **Moore/Sloan Data Science and WRF Innovation in Data Science Postdoctoral Fellow**, University of Washington.^[extracted: from the paper's author footnote]

## Why He Is Not the Interesting Name on This Paper

His three co-authors are: [[alex-damour|Alexander D'Amour]], [[daniel-cervone|Daniel Cervone]] and [[luke-bornn|Luke Bornn]].

**Cervone and Bornn are already in this vault** — both on [[expected-value-possession-framework|the EPV framework]], Bornn additionally on [[wide-open-spaces-space-creation|Wide Open Spaces]], the origin of the [[space-occupation-gain|SOG]] line.

> **The people who wrote the standard for testing metric quality also built two of the vault's football value surfaces, and reported none of these properties for them.** See `the-framework-and-the-metrics-share-authors-and-never-meet` on [[meta-analytics-sports-metrics]].

Franks himself is the vault's only *pure statistician* — a methodologist with no football paper here. That is the reason for the page: he is the link into a literature the vault needs and does not hold.

## The Prior Work This Paper Formalises

The paper is explicit that it is codifying material that **mostly existed in blogs**, naming FiveThirtyEight's Rob Arthur, Darryl Blackport on how long three-point shooting takes to stabilise, and Dawson Sprigings on NHL shooting.

> **The reliability question in sports analytics was worked out by practitioners before academics, and the academic paper's contribution is formalisation rather than discovery.**^[extracted: the authors state this] That is worth recording, because it suggests the football version may also exist informally — in club analytics departments and blogs — without appearing in the literature the vault searches.

The lineage he cites is older still: analysis of variance (Fisher, 1925), Cronbach's alpha (1951), Kuder–Richardson (1937). **These are psychometric tools roughly a century old**, arriving in sports analytics in 2016 and in football, so far as this vault holds, not at all.

## His Own Prior Football-Adjacent Work

Cited within: **Franks, Miller, Bornn & Goldsberry (2015)**, *Counterpoints: Advanced defensive metrics for NBA basketball*, MIT Sloan — a model-based defensive metric combining game-log and tracking data.
